# Supervising an agent

The practices that hold whatever the model or the harness. Written for the customer: the person who delegates work to the product and is responsible for what it does. Use them as the source for onboarding messages, help content, and the conversations on the floor.

## 1. Start where you can see everything

A new kind of work starts at propose or draft. You see what it would do before it does it. This is not distrust. It is how you learn what "right" looks like for this work, and how the agent learns from your edits.

What to tell customers: "For the first couple of weeks, it drafts and you send. You'll know when it's ready to send on its own, because you'll stop changing the drafts."

## 2. Edit, don't rewrite

When a draft is wrong, change the part that is wrong and send it. The edit is the most useful signal you can give. Rewriting from scratch hides what was wrong. Ignoring the draft and doing it yourself teaches nothing.

## 3. Correct the fact, not just the output

If the agent got something wrong because it believed something wrong ("Acme's billing contact is Dana"), fix the fact where the agent keeps its facts, not only the draft in front of you. Otherwise the same mistake comes back tomorrow. Ask the agent what it believes about your account. A good agent product shows you.

## 4. Let go on evidence

Move a kind of work up a rung when the record says you can: you have stopped editing, nothing has been reversed, nothing crossed a line. Not before, because you are busy. Not much later, because you are cautious. The product should offer the move with the evidence. If it does not, ask.

## 5. Know what cannot be undone

Some actions can be reversed in one step: a draft, a label, a scheduled send. Some cannot: a sent email, a payment, a deleted record. Before you let a kind of work act on its own, know which kind it is. Irreversible work stays where you see each one, or at most where you are told right after.

## 6. Know how to stop it

Find the stop control before you need it, and check that it works from wherever you will be: your phone, another machine, the middle of a meeting. A stop that does not stop is the most dangerous thing in the product.

## 7. Give it only what the job needs

Connect the systems, folders, and accounts the work needs, and no more. An agent with access to everything will eventually use something it should not. If the product asks for broad access "to be safe," ask what the work needs.

## 8. Rules belong in settings, not in chat

"Never email anyone in legal" typed into a conversation can be forgotten when the conversation gets long. The same rule set as a setting, a permission, or an approval rule is enforced every time. Put the rules you care about where the product enforces them. If the product has nowhere to put them, that is worth telling the vendor.

## 9. Read the receipt

When the agent tells you what it did while you were not watching, read it. It is how you notice drift before it becomes a mistake: more edits than last month, a kind of work you did not expect, a number that looks off.

## 10. Take it back without guilt

If the work starts going wrong, move it back down a rung. That is the system working, not failing. Tell the vendor why. A demotion with a reason is the fastest way back up.

## 11. You are still accountable

The agent acts for you. What it sends goes out in your name. Courts and customers have both said so. Supervision is not a tax on using the product. It is the job that replaced the work you delegated.
